Apricot Mallow

Apricot Mallow (Sphaeralcea ambigua), also known as desert globemallow, is a drought-tolerant perennial subshrub famous for its vibrant, bowl-shaped apricot-orange flowers.

White Clover

White Clover (Trifolium repens), is a herbaceous perennial plant in the bean family, Fabaceae. While Clover is technically a legume, it is widely recognized for its round, colorful flower heads. It serves as a vital, high-protein forage crop for both large and small mammalian herbivores across the North America continent.

What animals eat this plant?

Rabbits
Wild rabbits and hares are well-known clover consumers, relying on the tender leaves and flowers for essential moisture and nutrition.
White-Tailed Deer
These large herbivores frequently graze in open fields and forest clearings, favoring clover patches for their high protein content.
Cattle
Domestic cows routinely graze on red and white clover, which farmers often plant in pastures to boost dietary protein and improve milk production.
Honeybees
While not eating the foliage, these essential pollinators collect rich nectar and pollen directly from blooming clover flowers to make honey.
Caterpillars
Larvae of species like the Eastern Tails-Blue butterfly and Clover Loopers rely on clover plants as a primary host food source.
Canada Geese
These aquatic birds often forage in grassy fields, lawns, and parks, plucking young clover shoots as part of their herbivorous diet.

Coast Trillium

The Coast Trillium (Trillium ovatum), also known as the Pacific trillium, is a perennial wildflower native to the forests of western North America.

Emerges in early spring as an ephemeral, then dies back to the ground during summer dormancy. Opens bright white in the spring and gradually turns pink, rose, or deep purple as it ages.
